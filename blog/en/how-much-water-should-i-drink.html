How Much Water Should I Drink Per Day? A Complete Guide

March 15, 2026 6 min read Suu Team
The "8 glasses a day" rule is convenient but inaccurate for most people. Your actual daily water need depends on your weight, activity level, climate, and the beverages you consume. This guide gives you the science-backed formula plus a personalized intake table.

The Science-Based Formula

The most widely accepted guideline from nutritionists and sports scientists is to drink 30–40ml per kilogram of body weight per day under normal conditions.[1] This is far more accurate than a flat "8 glasses" recommendation.

Base daily water intake:

Body weight (kg) × 35ml = Daily intake (ml)

Example: 70kg person → 70 × 35 = 2,450ml per day

Adjusting for Activity and Climate

🏃

Exercise: Add 500–1,000ml per hour of activity

During physical exercise, you can lose 500–1,500ml per hour through sweat. Add this to your baseline. For intense or prolonged exercise, consider electrolyte drinks rather than plain water.

☀️

Hot Weather: Add 500ml in warm climates

In hot weather (above 25°C / 77°F), your body sweats more for temperature regulation. Add 500ml to your daily baseline. In very hot conditions (35°C+), add 1,000ml or more.

🤰

Pregnancy & Breastfeeding: +300–700ml above baseline

Pregnant women need about 300ml extra daily. Breastfeeding mothers need even more — up to 700ml above their normal baseline — since breast milk is about 87% water.

🤒

Illness: Increase significantly when sick

Fever, vomiting, and diarrhea cause significant fluid loss. When sick, drink as much water as possible and consider adding electrolytes. Dehydration during illness can worsen symptoms and slow recovery.

Does Coffee Count as Water?

Yes — but not 1:1. Caffeinated beverages like coffee have a mild diuretic effect, meaning they cause your kidneys to excrete slightly more water. However, modern research shows the net effect is still positive hydration: a 250ml coffee contributes roughly 150–200ml of net hydration.

⚠️ Warning Signs of Dehydration: Dark yellow urine, dry mouth, fatigue, headache, dizziness, and reduced cognitive performance. If your urine is pale yellow, you're well hydrated. If it's dark yellow or amber, drink water immediately.

You wake up 6–8 hours dehydrated. Drinking water first thing in the morning jumpstarts your metabolism and sets you up for a hydrated day.
