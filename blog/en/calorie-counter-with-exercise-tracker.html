Calorie Counter With Exercise Tracker: Which Apps Actually Connect the Two?

Published: 13 August 2026 · Furkan Mert Fındıklı · 8 min read

Short answer: Most apps that offer both simply place them side by side: workout calories get added to your daily allowance and nothing else changes. MyFitnessPal, Yazio and Lose It! all work this way. Samsung Health goes slightly further with ecosystem data. Suu is the one that connects them properly — the workout you log raises your hydration goal from estimated sweat loss, and your meal's protein and sodium add the water digestion requires on top.

Searching for a "calorie counter with exercise tracker" returns dozens of apps that technically qualify. Almost all of them log both things. The problem is that logging both is not the same as connecting both.

In the standard setup, your workout produces a number — say 600 kcal — which is added to your daily calorie allowance. That is the entire integration. Nothing else about your day changes: not your protein target, not your hydration, not your recovery.

That's fine if calories are all you care about. But three things move when you train hard, and only one of them is usually tracked.

What "connected" actually means

Three things change when you train hard, and a genuinely integrated app should react to all three:

  • Energy. You burned calories, so your intake target moves. Every app on this list does this.
  • Fluid. You lost water through sweat — often a litre or more on a long session in heat. No app on this list except Suu adjusts for it.
  • Digestion load. A high-protein, high-sodium recovery meal requires extra water to process. Nobody tracks this either, though it is well established physiologically.

Concrete example: 10 km run on a 30 °C day, followed by a high-protein dinner. MyFitnessPal adds ~600 kcal to your allowance and stops. Suu does the same, then raises the day's water goal twice — once for the sweat loss, once for the protein — and tells you why.

Where each competitor genuinely wins

Skipping this would make the guide useless, and these are real advantages:

  • MyFitnessPal — by far the largest food database. If you log packaged supermarket products, nothing else comes close. Note that barcode scanning now requires Premium.
  • Yazio — the strongest recipe and diet-plan library, plus a free tier that stays usable longer than most.
  • Lose It! — barcode scanning still on the free plan and a simple, fast logging flow. A common landing spot for people leaving MyFitnessPal.
  • Samsung Health — the widest activity list (90+) and excellent integration if you have a Galaxy Watch. Its food logging is the weakest part.
  • Suu — the connections described above, plus photo and voice calorie logging. Its food database is smaller and it has no barcode scanner or recipe library.

Does the hydration part actually matter?

It depends on how you train. For a 30-minute gym session in an air-conditioned room, fluid loss is modest and a fixed daily target is fine.

It starts to matter at longer durations and higher temperatures. Sweat rates during endurance exercise commonly run between 0.5 and 2 litres per hour depending on intensity, heat and individual physiology. On a hot long run, the deficit can exceed the entire fixed "2 litres a day" target most apps assume.

That is also the number that most directly affects how you feel the next day. If you have ever had a heavy training day followed by a headache and flat legs, under-replacement of fluid and electrolytes is a common cause — and it is the one variable none of the mainstream calorie apps track.

The two-app alternative, honestly assessed

Plenty of people run Strava plus MyFitnessPal, or Apple Fitness plus Cronometer. It works, and if you already have that habit there is no strong reason to break it.

Two caveats. First, the data flows one way: Apple Health or Health Connect can pass workout calories into a nutrition app, but the nutrition app does nothing clever with them beyond adjusting an allowance. Second, hydration sits outside both apps entirely, so the variable that moves most on a hard day is the one you are least likely to be tracking.

The case for one app is friction, not features: one log instead of two, and the connections drawn for you rather than in your head.

Verdict

If you want the biggest food database and calories are the priority: MyFitnessPal, accepting the barcode paywall.

If you want recipes and plans alongside logging: Yazio.

If you're deep in the Galaxy ecosystem: Samsung Health, for the activity breadth and watch integration.

If you want calories and exercise genuinely connected — where the workout changes your hydration target and the meal changes it again — Suu is the only app in this comparison that does it. That matters most if you train long, train in heat, or have noticed that hard training days leave you feeling worse than the calorie maths suggests they should.

Honest limitation: Suu has a smaller food database, no barcode scanner and no recipe library. If those are your priorities, pick one of the others.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an app that tracks calories and workouts together?

Many do: MyFitnessPal, Yazio, Lose It! and Samsung Health all log both. The difference is whether they connect them. In most, the workout only adds calories to your daily allowance. Suu additionally uses the workout to raise your hydration goal based on estimated sweat loss, and uses the meal's protein and sodium content to add the water digestion requires.

Which calorie counter has the best exercise tracking?

Samsung Health has the widest activity list (90+) but the weakest food logging. Suu offers around 50 activities with detailed running and cycling metrics alongside photo-based calorie logging. MyFitnessPal, Yazio and Lose It! all have basic exercise logging only.

Do I need two apps for calories and exercise?

No, but two apps works fine if you already have the habit. The trade-offs are that calorie data flows one way at best, and hydration falls outside both apps — meaning the variable that changes most on a hard training day goes untracked.

Does exercise really change how much water I need?

Yes, substantially. Sweat rates during endurance exercise commonly range from 0.5 to 2 litres per hour depending on intensity, heat and individual physiology. On a long session in warm weather the fluid deficit can exceed the fixed daily target most apps assume.

Which app is best if I want everything free?

Lose It! and Yazio have the most usable free tiers among the calorie-first apps, both including barcode scanning. Suu's free plan permanently includes water tracking across 91 beverages, exercise tracking and 3 AI analyses per day, but photo calorie analysis requires Premium and there is no barcode scanner.
