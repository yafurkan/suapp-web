The Best Photo Calorie App: 2026 Comparison

Published: 12 August 2026 · Furkan Mert Fındıklı · 7 min read

Short answer: If you only want to count calories, Cal AI and MyFitnessPal are strong picks, with MyFitnessPal holding the largest food database. If you want photo calorie analysis plus hydration and exercise in one app, Suu is the one that separates: on top of macro extraction it uses the same data to update your water goal from your workouts and your meal's digestion-water need.

This comparison was written by the Suu team — so you are reading a biased source. To offset that we have stated plainly where competitors beat Suu. Competitor features reflect publicly available information as of August 2026 and can change, since these apps ship updates frequently.

Photo-based calorie logging has been the fastest-spreading nutrition feature of the last two years. The idea is simple: you photograph your plate, a model recognises the food, estimates the portion and derives calories and macros. Compared with scanning barcodes or searching a database, the friction is close to zero.

But the apps differ meaningfully. Some use the photo as little more than a search shortcut, others genuinely estimate portions, and several put the feature behind a paywall entirely. And almost none of them use the nutrition data they extract to compute anything else.

Below we compare five apps with that in mind.

Setting the right expectation on accuracy

Let's be honest first: no app can derive gram-level calories from a photograph. The model recognises the food and estimates the portion. A photo cannot show the depth of rice in a covered bowl, how much oil is in a salad, or the fat content of a cut of meat.

That is not a flaw, it is the boundary of the method — and in practice it rarely matters. What determines success in weight management is not a ±15% error on a single meal, it is still logging weeks later. A perfect log abandoned on day three because manual search was tedious is far worse than an approximate log you actually keep.

One habit improves accuracy in every app: shoot at a slight angle with a known reference in frame (a fork, a glass), then correct the suggested portion once.

Where the competitors genuinely win

Skipping this would make the article useless:

  • MyFitnessPal — the largest food database in the category. Your odds of finding a branded supermarket product by barcode are highest here. If you have years of history there, migrating is a real cost.
  • Cal AI — the cleanest photo-first experience. It does one job and does it fast. If calories are all you track, there is nothing extra in the way.
  • Yazio — a strong recipe and diet-plan library. A genuine advantage if deciding what to cook is your bottleneck.
  • Lifesum — mature interface and habit coaching; its food-quality scoring is motivating for beginners.

Where Suu separates: putting the data to a second use

In the four apps above, the nutrition data extracted from your photo is written to a log line and stays there. In Suu the same data feeds a second calculation.

The protein and sodium content of the meal you logged determines the extra water digestion requires, and that is added to your daily hydration goal. In the same way, a run or ride you record raises the goal again based on estimated sweat loss. Ambient temperature is folded in as well.

So in Suu the water goal is not a fixed number — it is an output that moves with what you ate, how hard you trained and how hot the day is. Making that possible requires calories, exercise and hydration to live in one app; across three separate apps the link simply cannot be made.

Who it is not for: if you only count calories and hydration is not something you care about, this side of Suu is overhead — MyFitnessPal's database breadth will serve you better.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is calorie counting from a photo?

No app can produce gram-level accuracy from a photograph; the model recognises the food and estimates the portion. What matters in practice is not the error on a single meal but whether you keep logging. Correcting the suggested portion once noticeably improves accuracy.

Is there a free app that counts calories from a photo?

Photo analysis is costly to run, so most apps place it on a paid tier. Suu's free plan includes 3 AI analyses per day (voice or text); photo analysis is part of Premium, which can be tried free for 3 days. Core water tracking, the 91-beverage database and exercise tracking are permanently free.

What can I use instead of Cal AI?

Cal AI is calorie-only. If you want the same photo analysis alongside hydration and exercise tracking, Suu is the direct alternative; if you simply need a broader food database, MyFitnessPal is the better fit.

Are my photos stored?

In Suu, meal photos are not stored permanently after analysis. With voice entry, speech is converted to text by your device's operating system — the audio is never sent to Suu servers. There is no ad tracking and data is not shared with third parties.

Do the calories from a photo sync to Apple Health?

Yes. Suu syncs calorie and macro data two-way with Apple Health (iOS) and Google Health Connect (Android), so it stays consistent with your other health apps.
