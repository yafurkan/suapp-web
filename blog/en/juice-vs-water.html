Juice vs Water:
Which Is Healthier? Sugar, Vitamin and Hydration Comparison

Quick Comparison: Water vs Juice

💧 Plain Water

Hydration factor: 100% (reference)

  • 0 calories
  • 0 g sugar
  • 0 mg sodium
  • Dental health: neutral
  • Weight control: ideal
  • Diabetes risk: 0

🍊 Juice (240ml)

Hydration factor: ~75-80%

  • ~110-130 calories
  • ~21-26 g sugar
  • Vitamin C, polyphenols
  • Dental: acid erosion
  • Weight: hidden calorie category
  • Diabetes risk: high (packaged)

How Does Juice Affect Hydration?

Juice is about 85% water but doesn't act like water in the body:

  1. High sugar → osmotic effect: Blood sugar rises, kidneys use extra water to excrete excess sugar
  2. Increased urination: Sugar filtration increases urine production
  3. Net hydration: 240ml juice ≈ 180-190ml plain water hydration effect
  4. Duration effect: Blood sugar stays high for 30-60 min, additional thirst possible

Label Reading — Critical Tips

  • "100% juice": From fruit only, no added sugar (best packaged option)
  • "Nectar": Usually 25-50% fruit + 50-75% water + sugar — beware "from real fruit" labels
  • "Fruit drink": 5-10% fruit + sugar + flavoring — basically sugar water
  • "Light/Diet": Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame) — no sugar but artificial
  • "Ingredients" order: If first three include "sugar, glucose syrup, fructose" = ultra-sweet product

Juice Recommendations for Children

Updated American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommendations:

  • 0-12 months: ABSOLUTELY NO juice. Breast milk, formula, water sufficient.
  • 1-3 years: Daily max 120ml (1 small cup)
  • 4-6 years: Daily max 180ml
  • 7-18 years: Daily max 240ml

Golden rule: Whole fruit > squeezed juice. Eating 1 orange (fiber + satiety) > 1 cup OJ (no fiber, hidden calories). Suu kids profile gives juice sugar warning under 18.

Juice for Dieters

If weight loss is your goal, juice is RISKY:

  • 1 cup juice = ~110 kcal, no satiety → snack call
  • Same calories as whole fruit gives 30-40 min fullness
  • "Healthy" assumption → unchecked consumption — hidden calorie trap
  • Smoothies same category (fiber but still liquid calories)

Healthy Alternatives

  • Infused water: Add lemon, cucumber, mint, ginger, blueberries to plain water. 0 calories, 0 sugar, vitamins preserved. Suu Premium has infused water profiles.
  • Coconut water (fresh): ~45 kcal/240ml, natural electrolytes, sports usage
  • Whole fruit + 1 glass water: Fiber + hydration combo
  • Homemade lemonade (low sugar): 1 lemon + 250ml water + 1 tsp honey
  • Herbal tea (hot/cold): Mint, chamomile — caffeine-free, 0 sugar
