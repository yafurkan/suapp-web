Lifesum vs MyFitnessPal: Which Calorie App Should You Pick in 2026?

Published: October 14, 2026 · Furkan Mert Fındıklı · 7 min read

Short answer: MyFitnessPal is the better pick if database breadth and the odds of finding a branded product matter most: it has the largest database and community in the category, but the barcode scanner moved to the paid plan and the interface is busier. Lifesum wins if you want a cleaner, guided experience, diet plans and a free barcode scanner. Neither models hydration properly.

This comparison is published by Suu, which makes a competing app. That is a conflict of interest, so we have kept the Lifesum and MyFitnessPal sections free of Suu marketing and put our own app in a clearly separated section at the end, where you can skip it. Feature details reflect publicly available information as of October 2026 and change frequently — verify current plan limits on each app's store page.

MyFitnessPal is the best-known name in calorie counting; Lifesum is the cleaner, design-led alternative many people look at once MyFitnessPal feels too cluttered. Both count calories and macros, set diet goals and sync with health apps.

The real difference is emphasis: MyFitnessPal makes it easier to find every food, Lifesum makes it easier to keep the habit going.

Choose MyFitnessPal if you want to find every food

MyFitnessPal's strength is scale. Its database is the largest in the category and its community is huge, so your odds of finding a branded product, a restaurant dish or a regional recipe are the highest. If you eat out often or travel, that is a real advantage.

The trade-off is the free tier. The barcode scanner moved to the paid plan, the interface is busier and ads are more prominent, and photo meal logging is Premium too. If you plan to stay free, it's worth knowing those limits up front.

Choose Lifesum if you want a cleaner, guided experience

Lifesum's strength is the experience. The interface is uncluttered, onboarding is gentle, and the Life Score rates the quality of what you eat, not just the quantity — clearer feedback for a first-time tracker than a bare number.

Its diet plans and recipes answer the “what should I eat?” question, and the barcode scanner is still on the free plan. The trade-off: features such as customising your goals sit behind Premium, and the database isn't as broad as MyFitnessPal's.

Quick rule of thumb: if your problem is finding and logging everything you eat, MyFitnessPal. If your problem is sticking with it and deciding what to eat, Lifesum. If your problem is that food is only one part of the picture, keep reading.

What both of them leave on the table

Lifesum and MyFitnessPal are nutrition apps that added a water counter and a light exercise log. In practice two things go untracked in any meaningful way.

Hydration is a tally, not a model. Both count glasses without distinguishing what you drank — 250 ml of coffee and 250 ml of water are not the same for fluid balance, yet both apps count them the same and neither adjusts your target for a hot day.

Exercise does not feed anything. You can log a workout and your calorie allowance may rise, but the fluid you lost through sweat is not accounted for anywhere.

Verdict

MyFitnessPal — the largest database and community in the category. Best if you eat out a lot, buy many branded products and can live with ads or a paid barcode scanner.

Lifesum — cleaner interface, Life Score, diet plans, free barcode scanner. Best if you want a guided start.

Neither — if you want hydration modelled properly and your workouts to change your daily targets, both fall short by design, and that is the gap Suu is built for.

If you are choosing purely between these two and plan to stay free: try Lifesum first — the barcode scanner is still free there.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lifesum or MyFitnessPal better?

MyFitnessPal is better if database breadth and community matter; it has the largest database in the category. Lifesum is better if you want a cleaner, guided experience, diet plans and a free barcode scanner.

Is the MyFitnessPal barcode scanner free?

No. MyFitnessPal moved its barcode scanner to the paid plan; in Lifesum the barcode scanner is still available on the free plan.

Can you use Lifesum for free?

Yes — with Lifesum's free version you can log calories and macros and scan barcodes. Features such as customising goals, the full set of diet plans and detailed nutrient reports are Premium.

Do Lifesum and MyFitnessPal track water?

Both include a basic water counter, but neither models hydration properly: they don't distinguish what you drank and don't adjust your target for exercise or hot weather.

What is a cleaner alternative to MyFitnessPal?

Lifesum is a good alternative if you want a cleaner interface and a guided experience. If you want water, calories and exercise in one connected system, Suu is worth a look too.
