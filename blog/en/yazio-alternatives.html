The Best Yazio Alternatives in 2026: 5 Apps Compared

Published: October 3, 2026 · Furkan Mert Fındıklı · 10 min read

Short answer: There is no single best Yazio alternative — the right one depends on why you are leaving. MyFitnessPal has the largest food database, Lifesum the most polished diet plans, Cronometer the deepest vitamin and mineral tracking, and Lose It! a simple, budget-friendly logger with barcode scanning in the free tier. If you want calories, hydration and GPS workouts in one app and would rather say your meal than search for it, the strongest alternative is Suu: the run you record and the meal you log update your daily water goal automatically. If intermittent fasting and recipes are what you use most, staying on Yazio is a reasonable choice.

This comparison is written by the Suu team, so you are reading a biased source. To balance that, we state plainly where competitors are better: Yazio's fasting tracker and recipe library, MyFitnessPal's food database and Cronometer's micronutrient depth all go beyond what Suu offers. Features and free-tier limits reflect publicly available information as of October 2026; these apps change often, so check the store listing before you download.

The 5 best Yazio alternatives in 2026

Ranked not by feature count but by the reason you are leaving Yazio. All five count calories and macros correctly; the differences are how fast you can log a meal and how much the app knows about the rest of your day — what you drank and how you trained.

  1. Suu — best if you want calories, water and workouts in one app

    Free on iOS and Android. Say or type your meal — “two eggs, a slice of toast and a flat white” — and the AI extracts calories, protein, carbs and fat in seconds, and logs the coffee as a drink as well. On Premium you can simply photograph the plate. The real difference from Yazio is integration: Suu tracks 91 beverages with their own hydration factor, records runs, walks and rides live with GPS on a map, and the three feed each other — a logged run raises your water goal, a salty or protein-heavy meal adds digestion water. The free tier includes three AI analyses a day, and the Apple Watch app is free too. Honest limits: the branded-food database is smaller than MyFitnessPal's, and there is no recipe library or fasting timer. Get Suu free →

  2. MyFitnessPal — best food database, still

    The largest food and restaurant database in the category by a wide margin, built from millions of user entries. If you eat out a lot or buy many packaged products, nothing else finds your item as reliably. The costs: barcode scanning moved from the free tier to Premium, many entries are community-sourced and unverified, and water tracking is a plain cup counter. A sensible move if you want to swap Yazio's recipe-first approach for a data-first one. Head-to-head: Suu vs MyFitnessPal.

  3. Lifesum — best if you used Yazio for its plans

    Swedish-made and the most visually polished app on this list, built around lifestyle plans — keto, high-protein, intermittent fasting — with a rating system that grades the food you log. If plans were the reason you used Yazio, Lifesum will feel the most familiar. Most of the experience requires Premium, and its macro and micronutrient depth does not approach Cronometer's.

  4. Cronometer — best for micronutrient precision

    The serious choice if you track vitamins and minerals rather than just calories: verified data sources instead of crowd-sourced entries and 80+ micronutrients at gram level. Popular for clinical or protocol-driven tracking, and its free tier is generous. The interface is dense, there is no voice or photo logging, and it can feel too technical if all you want is a daily calorie number.

  5. Lose It! — best simple, budget-friendly logger

    A long-running US calorie counter with a straightforward interface, a large food database weighted towards American products and barcode scanning that stays in the free tier. Challenges with friends add some accountability. Fasting and more advanced planning sit behind Premium, hydration is basic, and it does not record GPS workouts itself — you would still pair it with a separate running app.

Why do people look for a Yazio alternative? Credit where it is due: Yazio is a good app. It is built in Erlangen, Germany, it is well localized, and its intermittent-fasting tracker and recipe library are among the best in the category. Most people who switch are not leaving a bad app; their needs have changed.

The sticking points usually fall into three groups: most recipes and plans sit behind PRO; logging a meal still means searching and entering grams; and water and exercise live apart from your calories. In Yazio, water is a basic counter — you see the same goal on the day you run 10 km and on the day you sit at a desk. As we explain in how to stick with calorie counting, that friction is exactly what makes people quit.

Why isn't Yazio's water tracking enough? How Suu's goal changes

In Yazio, water is a counter you tap through the day. But your fluid needs are not fixed: a 45-minute run, a 35 °C day or a salty dinner all change them. And not every drink counts as much as water.

In Suu the goal updates live. A 200 ml filter coffee counts as 160 ml of water at a factor of 0.8, black tea at 0.9, milk at 0.9. Cola is different: it counts at 0.6 and every 10 g of sugar raises your goal by 100 ml, because processing sugar takes extra water. And the moment a GPS run ends, the extra fluid you need is added to your goal.

  • Weather: your daily goal rises automatically as the temperature climbs.
  • Exercise: a workout recorded in Suu, or read from Apple Health / Health Connect, becomes extra fluid need.
  • Meals: a meal high in protein and sodium adds the water needed for digestion.
  • Suu Index: one 0–100 score that shows how well hydrated your day really was.

Curious about your own number? Start with the free water calculator, then get Suu free to watch the goal move through the day. The full 91-drink list is part of Premium; on the free plan, water and sparkling water are logged directly and other drinks by telling Suu AI.

Why does logging take so long? Voice and photo calorie tracking

The number one reason people stop counting calories is boredom: searching for every food, picking the right entry, guessing the grams. Research consistently shows that people who keep a food log lose more weight; the hard part is keeping it going for weeks.

In Suu you log a meal in one sentence: “Half a chicken burrito and a can of Diet Coke for lunch.” The AI separates the food from the drink and records calories, macros and the drink's hydration contribution at once. On Premium you just photograph the plate. Speech is converted to text by your phone's operating system — the audio is never sent to Suu's servers — and meal photos are not stored permanently.

Which Yazio alternative also tracks runs and workouts?

In Yazio you enter workouts by hand or import them from a health app; it does not record your run with GPS itself. That is why many people end up running Yazio next to a separate running app and a separate water app — three apps, three goals that know nothing about each other.

Suu records about 50 activities. For runs, walks and rides you get live GPS tracking: distance, time, pace and average speed update during the workout, elevation shows on rides, and the route is drawn on the map when you finish. As soon as you stop, the calories burned go into your daily balance and the extra fluid need goes into your water goal. With an Apple Watch you can record the same workout from your wrist using the watch's GPS and live heart rate — and the Apple Watch app works fully on the free plan.

If intermittent fasting and recipes are your priority: stay with Yazio — it is still the strongest app on this list for both. Lifesum is the closest alternative.
