Why You Should Drink Water First Thing in the Morning

March 15, 2026 6 min read Suu Team
Every morning you wake up in a mild state of dehydration. You haven't drunk anything in 7–8 hours, your body has been quietly burning through fluid all night, and the first thing most people do is reach for coffee. There's a better opening move — and the science behind it is more compelling than you might expect.

Why You Wake Up Dehydrated

During a typical night's sleep of 7–8 hours, your body loses approximately 0.5 litres of water. This happens through two main routes: breathing (you exhale water vapour with every breath, even while asleep) and perspiration (your skin releases moisture continuously, not just when you're visibly sweating).

Add to this the fact that most people don't drink anything in the 2–3 hours before bed, and you can see why waking up mildly dehydrated is the physiological default — not an exception. At this point, your body's fluid balance is already tipped, your blood is marginally more concentrated, your cells are calling for water, and your brain — which is about 73% water — is running a little low.

The good news is that this is one of the easiest hydration deficits to fix. A glass of water within minutes of waking starts rehydrating your body immediately. No special preparation, no cost, no effort — and the benefits are immediate and cumulative.

7 Science-Backed Benefits of Drinking Water in the Morning

⚡

Metabolism Boost

Drinking 500 ml of water on an empty stomach has been shown in clinical research to increase metabolic rate by 24–30% for 30–40 minutes.[1] This is known as water-induced thermogenesis. Your body uses energy to warm the water to body temperature, temporarily elevating calorie burn.

🫀

Toxin and Waste Flush

Your kidneys filter waste products from your blood continuously, excreting them in urine. After a night without fluid, your body is primed to flush out overnight waste products as soon as you provide the water to do so. Morning water jumpstarts kidney function and supports your body's natural detox processes.

🍎

Better Digestion

Water on an empty stomach stimulates the production of digestive enzymes and gastric acids, preparing your gut for the day's first meal. It also rehydrates the mucous membranes of the digestive tract, which helps move food through more smoothly and can reduce bloating and constipation.

🔋

Increased Energy

One of the first organs to feel fluid deficit is the brain. Rehydrating promptly after waking helps restore full cognitive function, mood, and alertness. Many people who report feeling groggy in the morning are partially dehydrated — and a glass of water can reduce that grogginess faster than the first coffee.

✨

Skin Health

Chronic mild dehydration is one of the most underappreciated contributors to dull, tired-looking skin. Properly hydrated skin cells appear fuller, more elastic, and more radiant. Drinking water first thing helps restore hydration to your skin cells after their overnight deficit — a simple and entirely free skincare step.

🧠

Sharper Focus

Studies from the University of East London and the University of Westminster found that drinking water before mental tasks improved performance and reaction times. Even mild dehydration impairs short-term memory and concentration.[2] Morning water primes your brain for a more focused, productive start to the day.

❤️

Heart Health

A study published in the American Journal of Epidemiology found that people who drank five or more glasses of water per day had a significantly lower risk of coronary heart disease compared to those who drank two or fewer glasses. Starting your count first thing in the morning makes hitting that daily target far easier.

How Much to Drink — and When

The most effective morning hydration routine is simpler than most people expect. The goal is not to chug an enormous quantity of water immediately — it's to start rehydrating your body before you reach for coffee, food, or anything else.

Recommended morning dose: Drink 250–500 ml of water within the first 15–30 minutes of waking, before your morning coffee or breakfast. Room temperature or warm water is absorbed slightly faster than cold and tends to feel more comfortable on an empty stomach.

If 500 ml feels like too much first thing in the morning, start with 250 ml and build the habit from there. The important thing is consistency — drinking water every morning, regardless of the quantity, is far more beneficial than occasionally drinking a large amount and skipping most days.

Once the habit is established, 250–500 ml in the morning makes a meaningful contribution to your daily goal. If your total daily target is 2.5 litres, morning water represents 10–20% of that target — achieved before your day has even properly begun.

What About Adding Lemon?

The "lemon water in the morning" trend has genuine roots in practicality, even if some of its claimed benefits are exaggerated. Here's what the evidence actually supports:

  • Vitamin C: A squeeze of lemon adds a small but real dose of vitamin C and antioxidants.
  • Flavour: If lemon makes plain water more appealing and encourages you to drink more, it's a net positive.
  • Digestive stimulation: The acidity may mildly stimulate digestive juices, though this effect is small.
  • Detox claims: The liver and kidneys handle detoxification — lemon water doesn't meaningfully accelerate this beyond what plain water does.
  • Tooth enamel: Daily lemon juice contact with teeth can erode enamel over time. Use a straw or rinse your mouth with plain water afterwards.

Bottom line: lemon water is a pleasant variation that adds some benefit. But plain water does the heavy lifting. Don't skip morning water if you don't have a lemon handy.
