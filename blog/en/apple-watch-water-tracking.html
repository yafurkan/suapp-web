Why Apple Watch Data Matters for Hydration

Sweat loss during exercise varies enormously — intensity, duration, ambient temperature and body mass all change how much fluid you need to replace. A static "drink 2 litres a day" target ignores all of it.

Your Apple Watch already measures the inputs that matter: workout type, duration, active energy and heart rate. The question is whether your hydration app does anything with them. That is the difference this setup makes.

How to Connect Your Apple Watch to Suu

  1. Download Suu from the App Store on your iPhone and fill in height, weight, gender and activity level — the goal calculation depends on it. Then install Suu on your Apple Watch from the Watch app on your iPhone.
  2. Enable Apple Health read access for Workouts, Steps and Active Energy in Settings → Apple Health.
  3. Enable write access for Water and Nutrition so your Suu entries appear in the Health app.
  4. Record your workout in the Suu Watch app — or in Apple's own Workout app. Suu updates your goal when it ends.
  5. Log water from your wrist in the Suu Watch app, or with Siri: "Add 500 ml of water to Suu."

💡 Pro tip: Add Suu's Lock Screen widget on your iPhone. The new goal — recalculated the moment your Apple Watch workout ends — is visible without unlocking.

When This Setup Actually Changes Things
