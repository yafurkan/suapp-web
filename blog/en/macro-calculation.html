How to Calculate Macros: Protein, Carbs and Fat Explained

July 3, 2026 7 min read Furkan Mert Fındıklı

Counting calories alone isn't enough — two diets with the same calories but different macro splits produce very different results in your body. When you set your macros (protein, carbs, fat) correctly, you stay fuller, protect muscle, and reach your goal faster. This guide shows you how to calculate your macros from scratch, step by step.

What Are Macronutrients?

Macronutrients are the three main nutrients your body needs in large amounts for energy. Each has a fixed calorie value per gram:

MacroCalories / gramMain role
Protein4 kcalMuscle repair, satiety, immunity
Carbohydrate4 kcalPrimary energy, training performance
Fat9 kcalHormones, vitamin absorption, cell structure

Your total calorie intake equals the sum of these three macros. "Counting macros" means looking not just at total calories, but at how those calories are distributed.

🧮 Practical shortcut

To maintain weight, roughly body weight (kg) × 30–33 kcal is a good starting point (moderate activity). For a 70 kg person that's ≈ 2,100–2,300 kcal. To lose weight, subtract 300–500 kcal per day.

Step 2: Split Calories Into Macros

Order matters: set protein first, then fat, then put the remaining calories into carbs.

1

Set Protein First

For an active person aim for 1.6–2.2 g/kg. Example: 70 kg × 1.8 = 126 g protein. That's 126 × 4 = 504 kcal. Protein comes first because it keeps you full and reduces muscle loss.

2

Set Fat

Let 20–35% of total calories come from fat (minimum ~0.5–0.8 g/kg for hormones). On a 2,400 kcal day, 30% = 720 kcal → 720 ÷ 9 = 80 g fat.

3

Put the Rest Into Carbs

Subtract protein and fat calories from your total, then divide by 4. 2,400 − 504 − 720 = 1,176 kcal → 1,176 ÷ 4 = 294 g carbs.

Macro Splits by Goal

Total calories determine the outcome, but the split makes it easier:

  • Fat loss: high protein (1.8–2.2 g/kg), fat 20–30%, rest carbs — for satiety and muscle retention.
  • Muscle gain: slight surplus (+250–400 kcal), higher carbs (training fuel).
  • Maintenance/health: balanced — protein 1.6 g/kg, fat 30%, rest carbs.

💡 Remember

As long as protein and total calories stay fixed, shifting the carb/fat ratio won't meaningfully change your weight result. So pick the split you can sustain.

Summary

  • Macros: protein (4 kcal/g), carbs (4 kcal/g), fat (9 kcal/g).
  • Find daily calories first, then split protein → fat → carbs in that order.
  • Protein: 1.6–2.2 g/kg · Fat: 20–35% of calories · Carbs: the remainder.
  • With protein and total calories fixed, prioritize a split you can sustain.
