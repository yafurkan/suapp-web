Does Coffee Really Dehydrate You?

What research says, and why Suu still counts drinks cautiously.

Not in moderate amounts. In a study of 50 men who usually drank 3–6 cups a day, drinking 4 × 200 ml of coffee a day for three days made no difference to body water or hydration markers compared with the same amount of water.1 In a trial comparing different drinks, milk and an oral rehydration solution were retained longer than water, while coffee, tea and cola were not clearly different from water.2

How Suu counts drinks

Suu counts each drink with a hydration factor: how much of what you drink is added to your water goal. 200 ml of filter coffee × 0.8 = 160 ml of water. The factors are deliberately cautious; they account for strong or large coffees, caffeine sensitivity and sugar.

Sugar raises your goal, not caffeine

Suu raises your daily water goal by 100 ml for every 10 g of sugar in a drink. Caffeine does not raise it. A Grande Frappuccino has about 64 g of sugar, so your goal goes up by about 640 ml.

⚡

The caffeine limit

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) considers up to 400 mg of caffeine a day safe for healthy adults, no more than 200 mg at once, and no more than 200 mg a day in pregnancy.3 In Suu's data a Grande cold brew has about 237 mg.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does coffee really dehydrate you?
Not in moderate amounts. In a study of 50 regular coffee drinkers, 4 × 200 ml of coffee a day hydrated as well as the same amount of water. Suu still counts plain coffee cautiously (filter coffee 0.8, Americano and cold brew 0.75), so a Grande Americano counts as about 355 ml of water.
How much water should I drink after Starbucks?
After a plain coffee, no extra glass is needed: the coffee itself counts toward your water goal. Sweetened drinks are different: Suu raises your daily goal by 100 ml for every 10 g of sugar, so a Grande Caramel Macchiato (about 34 g of sugar) adds about 340 ml.
Which Starbucks drink raises your water needs the most?
Sweet blended drinks: a Grande Frappuccino with about 64 g of sugar raises your goal by about 640 ml, and only 237 ml of it counts as water. White Chocolate Mocha is next at about 530 ml. A plain Americano or cold brew does not raise it at all.
How many coffees can I drink per day?
EFSA considers up to 400 mg of caffeine a day safe for healthy adults, and up to 200 mg in pregnancy. In Suu's data a Grande cold brew has about 237 mg and a Grande Americano about 213 mg, so two Grande cold brews get close to the limit. Energy drinks and cola count toward the same total.
Does Suu have Nevada Coffee and Greenwich drinks?
Yes. Suu's drink list includes 11 Starbucks, 8 Nevada and 8 Greenwich drinks, each with its own hydration factor, sugar and caffeine. The full list is Premium; on the free plan you can log water and sparkling water from the list, and other drinks by telling the assistant Suui.
How accurate are these values?
They are the values the Suu app uses, based on typical recipes. Real drinks vary with size, extra shots and syrup; sugar and caffeine are per-100 ml averages. For exact numbers, check the chain's own nutrition information.
